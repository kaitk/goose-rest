# 040: Rate gaps in the v0.1.0 benchmark session

Status: in-progress

The 2026-10-15 Goose-only session (`bench/all.sh`, goose-rest `v0.1.0`, goose-http
`v0.3.3`, Goose `v2026.1`) closed the serialized-JSON, memory, and upload gaps, but left
three results that didn't follow goose-http's gains. Find what causes each, and fix what
belongs to goose-rest.

Each comparison crosses two sessions on the same desktop: the earlier one ran goose-rest
`v0.0.1`, goose-http `v0.3.1`, and Goose `bc3c1f1` (four commits before `v2026.1`, the
same compiler code). Rates are medians of 3 runs, 4 workers; instruction counts are one
run each, from `bench/rest.sh count` (one worker on the epoll loop). Sources:
`bench/results/2026-10-15T2102-rest-Tiamat/` and goose-http's
`bench/results/2026-10-15T1539-rest-Tiamat/` and `2026-10-14T2039-rest-Tiamat-frm/`.

## 1. Tier A on epoll, the largest gap

| Tier A serialized, req/s | goose-http alone | goose-rest | gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| epoll, earlier session | 432 K | 430 K | -0.5% |
| epoll, this session | 454 K (453.0-454.2) | 432 K (426.5-433.6) | -4.9% |
| io_uring, earlier session | 586 K | 582 K | -0.8% |
| io_uring, this session | 600 K | 584 K | -2.7% |

goose-http alone gained 5% on epoll and goose-rest stayed level. The instructions don't
explain it: on `GET /api/items/1`, goose-rest's extra instructions fell from 178 to 145
(4,229 against 4,084), but its user cycles are 12% higher (1,656 against 1,474) for 3.5%
more instructions. goose-rest loses on instructions per cycle, not on work.

- [x] Compare the counters and find where the cycles go (2026-10-16). One worker on
      the epoll loop, h2load as `count.sh` runs it, 4 million requests of
      `GET /api/items/1`, per request: goose-http alone 1,456 user cycles, 4,084
      instructions, 13.5 L1d misses, 1.0 branch miss, 1.4 L1i misses; goose-rest at
      `a2b1af0` 1,623 cycles, 4,229 instructions, 17.5 L1d misses, 1.2 and 1.5. No
      function has a stack frame past a page (`tier_a` holds 2.2 KB), so section 2's
      cause isn't this one. goose-rest's extra is spread over its own work: the route
      function and answer, 515 cycles against goose-http's handler and answer's 373,
      `rest::json_bytes` and `rest::param`, 61, against a router 79 cycles lighter.
- [x] Whether goose-rest got slower: no. `v0.0.1`'s tier server on goose-http `v0.3.1`
      and `a2b1af0`'s on `v0.3.3`, both built with `v2026.1`: tier A's item 1,683 cycles
      against 1,618, its 401 1,630 against 1,658, its 404 1,247 against 1,233. goose-http
      alone gained on epoll in `v0.3.3`, and goose-rest's gap is its own overhead,
      about 160 cycles a request, which the earlier session's rates didn't separate.
- [ ] Open, for discussion: whether to cut that overhead. It's optimization, not a
      regression; the profile above says where it is.

## 2. The generated router, about 105 instructions more per request

| Tier R | earlier session | this session |
|---|---:|---:|
| Generated router, io_uring req/s | 462 K | 447 K (-3.2%) |
| Generated router, epoll req/s | 358 K | 354 K (-1.1%) |
| Generated, route 1, instructions | 2,852 | 2,958 (+106) |
| Generated, route 201, instructions | 3,299 | 3,403 (+104) |
| If chain, route 1, instructions | 3,168 | 3,195 (+27) |
| If chain, io_uring req/s | 323 K | 333 K (+3.1%) |

The same increase at route 1 and route 201 puts the cost outside the route walk, in
what the generated handlers or their dispatch do per request since `v0.0.1`. Its cycles
are also high for its instructions: 2,250 user cycles on route 1, against the if
chain's 1,619 for 3,195 instructions.

- [x] Find the ~105 instructions. The router's source didn't change; its stack frame
      did. Goose inlines every route's `rest::route` into `dispatch` and declares all
      their temporaries at the C function's top, among them a copy of `rest::Cx`, about
      170 bytes, for each call that takes it, so `dispatch`'s frame was 143 KB on
      `v0.0.1` and 217 KB on `v0.1.0`, whose `Cx` is larger. gcc's
      `-fstack-clash-protection`, Ubuntu's default, probes that frame a page at a time
      on every call: 53 pages, the 106 instructions, and 56% of the server's user cycles
      (`perf record`; L1d misses 125 to 165 a request). Built with
      `-fno-stack-clash-protection`, route 1 took 2,744 instructions and 923 cycles
      against 2,958 and 2,220. Recorded in `docs/goose-findings.md`.
- [x] Fix it: the generator writes each operation as its own function, which Goose
      keeps out of line (`a2b1af0`). `dispatch`'s frame is 74 KB, its path matching's.
      Route 1: 2,829 instructions and 1,022 cycles, against `v0.0.1`'s 2,852 and 1,583;
      route 201: 3,314 and 1,237, against 3,299 and 1,995. One run each, 2026-10-16.
- [ ] Open, for discussion: the hand-written chain, `bench/rest/goose-rest/github_app.goose`,
      keeps a 190 KB frame, as any program with many routes in one function would. Its
      shape stands for a user's chain, so it isn't changed to dodge the cost; whether
      the docs should advise a function per route, or `rest::Cx` should shrink, is open.

## 3. Tier M's stack, mostly tier A's gain

On io_uring, tier A rose from 578 K to 593 K and tier M stayed at 494 K, so the stack's
share grew from 14.5% to 16.7%. On epoll it fell from 17.9% to 17.0%. The stack's
instructions on a 401 rose only from 1,313 to 1,339. Lowest priority: look at it after
1, since tier M is tier A plus the stack and may share its cause.

- [x] After 1, count tier M: 1,848 cycles on `v0.0.1`'s build and 1,888 on
      `a2b1af0`'s, 5,830 instructions against 5,866, within run spread. Its share follows
      tier A: no regression of its own. The stack's cost against dusty-routes's is
      optimization, open with section 1's.
